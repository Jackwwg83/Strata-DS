# K11: the CPU EXL3 expert kernel, faster (AVX2 first)

CI-TARGETS: k11_cpu_moe_test ds41_generate
CI-TEST: K11_WEIGHTS=all ./k11_cpu_moe_test
CI-FILES: third_party/exllamav3_moe/ ds41/tasks/K11.
CI-ISSUE: 9

## Why

The routed experts that miss the VRAM cache run on the CPU, in exllamav3's CPU kernel (`moe_mul1`, vendored in
`third_party/exllamav3_moe/`). On the queue box (Intel i9-14900K: AVX2, no AVX-512) the CPU experts take about 90% of a
decode step: about 0.6 ms per expert with the weights in RAM, about 22 GB/s, against 44 GB/s for the same kernel with
AVX-512 on a Ryzen 7950X (2026-10-05). Most PCs with an Intel CPU have no AVX-512. The decode speed of the whole engine
on those PCs is this kernel's speed. Measurements: `ds41/bench/results/2026-10-06-m4/README.md`.

## What it computes

`exl3_moe_cpu_forward_raw` in `third_party/exllamav3_moe/moe_mul1.h` (the engine's only call): for m tokens (1..8) and
6 selected experts each, gate/up (5120 -> 2304), SwiGLU with limit 10, down (2304 -> 5120), weighted sum, FP32 output.
Keep the interface (`moe_mul1.h` declarations) unchanged; the engine also uses `exl3_moe_cpu_make_layer_raw` and
`exl3_moe_cpu_set_expert_raw`.

## Acceptance (`src/ds41/tests/k11_cpu_moe_test.cpp`, fixed)

- Inputs: the K10 golden (32 real experts of layer 10, m = 1, 4, 8; `ds41/ci/make_k10_golden.py`), experts copied to RAM.
- Accuracy: relative L2 against the FP16 golden at most 0.0325 for every m. The vendored kernel quantizes activations to
  int8 and measures 0.0279 / 0.0289 / 0.0295; 0.0325 is that plus 10%. Better accuracy is welcome, not required.
- Speed: median of 21 forwards at m = 1 and m = 8 with 8 threads (`K11_THREADS`): `score_us = us_m1 + us_m8 / 8`.
  Baseline on the queue box: us_m1 3573, us_m8 19306, score_us 5987.
- `ds41_generate` must still build (CI-TARGETS).
- The queue also runs the file-backed diagnostic modes (`K11_WEIGHTS=all`: the experts read through the mapped
  `experts.bin` with warm page tables, fresh page tables, and a dropped page cache). They are not scored, but the
  reviewer merges a variant only if it is not slower than the control in `mmap-ptecold` and `mmap-cold`: the engine
  reads about a third of its CPU experts this way (K11-04 won `ram` and lost 12-17% in the engine for this reason).

## Rules

1. Change only `third_party/exllamav3_moe/` (and the task notes `ds41/tasks/K11.*`). Keep the unmodified-vendor history:
   new code in new files under `third_party/exllamav3_moe/` or as clearly marked Strata-DS patches.
2. The kernel must keep working on CPUs without AVX-512 and on CPUs with AVX-512 (runtime dispatch, as it does now). The
   queue box measures AVX2 only; do not slow the AVX-512 path (the reviewer checks it on another box before merging).
3. No new dependencies. C++17. Threads: the existing pool (`num_threads` argument); on hybrid Intel CPUs the engine
   passes the P-core count (8 here): 16 threads measured slower (5614 us at m = 1).
4. The general rules of `ds41/tasks/README.md` apply where they fit (fixed interface and test, no numerics changed to
   pass). Rule 7 (CUDA graphs) does not apply: this is CPU code.

## Hints

The kernel is memory-bound only at 44 GB/s; at 22 GB/s on AVX2 the trellis decode is likely the limit. Measure first
(perf, or timers around decode vs. dot products). Ideas: cheaper AVX2 decode of the 3-bit mul1 trellis, better cache
blocking of the activation, fewer pool barriers per expert at m = 1, splitting work so 8 threads all stay busy.
