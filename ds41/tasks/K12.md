# K12: routed experts for prefill (many tokens, rows grouped by expert)

CI-TARGETS: ds41_k12_exl3_moe_prefill k12_exl3_moe_prefill_test
CI-TEST: ./k12_exl3_moe_prefill_test
CI-FILES: src/ds41/kernels/k12_exl3_moe_prefill.cu src/ds41/kernels/k12/ third_party/exllamav3_gpu/ ds41/tasks/K12.

## Why

Prefill (M3, `ds41/docs/m3-plan.html`) runs a chunk of up to 8192 prompt tokens through each layer at once. Each token
routes to 6 of 384 experts, so a 4096-token chunk makes 24576 (token, expert) rows, about 64 per expert. K10
(`exl3_moe_decode`) computes at most 8 tokens per call and reads each expert's weights once per call: 4096 tokens take
1.74 s per layer on an RTX 3060 (40 layers: 70 s). A kernel that reads each expert once and multiplies all its rows
(a GEMM, not a GEMV) is about 25x faster at this size: 1.74 TFLOP per layer at 4096 tokens, about 70 ms on the 3060.

## What it computes

Interface: `include/strata/ds41/kernels/k12_exl3_moe_prefill.hpp` (`exl3_moe_prefill`,
`exl3_moe_prefill_workspace_bytes`). The math per row is exactly K10's (read `ds41/tasks/K10.md` and the K10
implementation `src/ds41/kernels/k10_exl3_moe.cu` + `src/ds41/kernels/k10/pipeline.cuh`): input Hadamard with suh,
EXL3 mul1 3-bit trellis GEMM, output Hadamard with svh; `g = min(g, 10)`, `u = clamp(u, -10, 10)`,
`h = bf16(silu(g) * u * w)`, FP8 block-32 quantize-dequantize of h, `fp16(h)`, down projection, `out[tok] += ...`.

The caller (the engine) sorts the rows by expert and calls once per group of experts that is resident in VRAM
(the engine streams experts into VRAM about 64 at a time). Group offsets are host-side, so the launch shapes are
known without a device-to-host copy.

## How

1. Reuse exllamav3's GEMM path for many rows: `exllamav3_ext/quant/exl3_gemm*` (`exl3_gemm`, `exl3_mgemm`: multi-matrix
   GEMM with index lists) and its kernel instances (`comp_units/`), or the reconstruct path (`reconstruct.cu`:
   dequantize a whole matrix to fp16, then cuBLAS `cublasGemmEx`, as exllamav3's `BlockSparseMLP` does for experts
   with many rows). Pick what is faster at 16-512 rows per expert; you can combine both (by row count, as
   exllamav3 does). Source: `~/projects/exllamav3` at commit 16a49792 (v1.5.4). The K10 vendoring in
   `third_party/exllamav3_gpu/` (README, `strata.patch`, `UPSTREAM.sha256`) shows the pattern: copy the files
   unmodified, then patch in separate commits. Extend that directory; do not break K10, which includes files from it.
2. The activation step between the gate/up and down projections (clamp, SwiGLU, weight, bf16, FP8 block-32
   quantize-dequantize, fp16) is not in exllamav3: write it as K10 does (`k10/pipeline.cuh`).
3. Gather the rows' inputs `x[tok[r]]` into the workspace; scatter-add the outputs with atomics or in a fixed order.
4. CMake is fixed: `ds41_k12_exl3_moe_prefill` builds only `src/ds41/kernels/k12_exl3_moe_prefill.cu` and links
   `CUDA::cublas`. Compile vendored .cu files by #including them (unity build), as K10 does.

## Acceptance (`src/ds41/tests/k12_exl3_moe_prefill_test.cu`, fixed; runs on a GPU, no files needed)

- 384 synthetic experts (random trellis, random signed scales, 3 bits per weight, tile_w 48), about 5.1 GB of VRAM.
- Reference: K10 run 8 tokens per call. Relative L2 of the output change at most 1e-2 in three cases: T = 1 with 384
  groups (most empty); T = 37 over 64 experts with two tokens that have no rows (their output rows must stay
  bit-identical); T = 512 with skewed routing, 6 calls of 64 experts (off[0] > 0 in calls 2-6).
- Why 1e-2 and not K10's 5e-3: K10's GEMV adds partial sums in FP16; a GEMM with FP32 accumulation is at least as
  exact, but the two differ slightly in g and u, and the bf16 and FP8 (3 mantissa bits) roundings of h turn some of
  those differences into whole rounding steps. Measured on the RTX 3060 with the reconstruct + cuBLAS version
  (2026-10-06): 3.9e-3 / 6.0e-3 / 6.2e-3 against K10; 1.6e-3 for all three with the FP8 step removed from both
  kernels (an experiment, not committed). For scale: decode's CPU experts (int8 activations) differ from the FP16
  path by 2.9e-2.
- Rule 7 graph check (T = 37): one-time setup at the first call is allowed; after it, no allocation and no host sync.
- Ranking: `score_us` = median time of the 6 calls for a 4096-token chunk (skewed routing). Also reported: `us_t512`
  and, for comparison, K10's loop (`k10_loop_us_t4096`, 1.74e6 on the RTX 3060).
- Compiles for sm_86, sm_89, sm_120 (CUDA 12.8).

The committed placeholder computes nothing and fails.
